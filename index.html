Energized line crossings (optional)

Checks clearance between your wire and each energized line it passes over. Add one for each line. Needs the heights filled in.

Line height is the top energized conductor above the ground at the crossing. Picking a voltage fills in the OSHA 1926.1408 Table A distance; change it to the utility's or contractor's minimum approach distance.
Obstacles (optional)

Roads, rail, fences, buildings, trees or anything else the wire has to clear. Needs the heights filled in.

Height is the top of the obstacle above the ground. Width is optional; leave blank for a single point. Clearance blank = your desired ground clearance.

Assumptions: exact catenary shape for a hanging wire, tension dropping at each block by the block efficiency entered (multi-span helicopter pulls), a Helistringer brake that holds a fixed torque so wire tension rises as the reel empties, a straight long line, and no wind, ice, rotor wash or aircraft motion. Wire in span is the wire hanging between attachments only; add wire for tie-offs, dead-ends, sagging and reserve on the reel. Load limits must come from the aircraft flight manual supplement for the equipment installed. Keep pulling line tension within a 5:1 design factor of its rated breaking strength and within the rated working load of grips, swivels and connectors. Keep conductor tension under 10% of its rated breaking strength (IEEE 524) to avoid pre-stressing it.

References: IEEE Std 524-2016, Guide for the Installation of Overhead Transmission Line Conductors (Annex F block efficiency, reel braking tension, helicopter pulls, grounding and crossing structures); OSHA 29 CFR 1926.1408 Table A (minimum approach distances); FAA AC 133-1B, Rotorcraft External-Load Operations.
